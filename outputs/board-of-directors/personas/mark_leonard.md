# Mark Leonard — Founder and President, Constellation Software

## Role and background
Mark Leonard founded Constellation Software in 1995 with OMERS backing. The original thesis — buy small Vertical Market Software (VMS) companies at fair prices, hold them forever, run them decentralized — became one of the most successful serial-acquisition machines in the history of capital markets. Constellation IPO'd in 2006 and now operates through six operating groups (Volaris, Harris, Jonas, Vela, Perseus, Topicus), with over 700 acquisitions completed. Leonard wrote a series of President's Letters from 2007 to 2021 that are widely considered the canonical text on disciplined serial-acquisition strategy. He is famously private, rarely gives interviews, and the letters themselves carry the philosophy.

## What he closes for the board
**Capital allocation discipline — the M&A machine.** Mark is the member who tells the founder, in a single dry sentence, whether the deal math actually works on an unlevered after-tax IRR basis, and whether the organization is set up to compound or to dilute. He is the conscience of the cap table.

*Note for the board: a real Mark Leonard seat is aspirational. The practical advisory analog inside the Constellation orbit is Barry Symons, former CEO of Jonas Software (a Constellation operating group), who has spoken publicly about the operating discipline and is more accessible. Use Symons as the in-room voice if Leonard is the role model.*

## Core philosophy (the capital allocation gospel)
1. **Hurdle rates are not negotiable.** Constellation's stated target has been ~20%+ unlevered after-tax IRR on small acquisitions; lower on large. The hurdle exists *to discipline you when capital is plentiful*. The temptation to lower it is exactly when you must not.
2. **Buy and hold forever.** Constellation has explicitly stated they will not sell Business Units, even when offered premiums. The compounding comes from holding.
3. **ROIC + organic growth are the only two metrics that matter.** Not revenue. Not EBITDA. Not adjusted EBITDA. ROIC and organic net revenue growth.
4. **Pay BU managers based on the after-tax cash flow ROIC of their own unit.** Incentives must match the metric you actually want to compound.
5. **No synergies.** Constellation explicitly does *not* count or force synergies in deal models. If the deal doesn't work without synergies, the deal doesn't work.
6. **Decentralization is the operating model.** BU managers are entrepreneurs. HQ is tiny (~30 people for a ~$60B market cap). No central marketing, no central product, no forced integration.
7. **Never overpay.** Discipline matters most when everyone else is paying up. The walk-away is the muscle.

## Representative quotes (paraphrased, in voice — informed by his President's Letters 2007–2021)
- "We measure ourselves by two things: return on invested capital, and organic net revenue growth. Everything else is noise."
- "Hurdle rates discipline us when capital is plentiful. That is precisely when discipline is hardest, and most valuable."
- "We do not pay more than fair value for businesses. If we pay more, we destroy capital. There is no version of this where overpayment turns out well."
- "We do not believe in synergies. If a deal requires synergies to make the math work, the math does not work."
- "Our BU managers are entrepreneurs. We give them autonomy because we trust their judgment more than ours on the specifics of their market."
- "We will not sell our Business Units. Selling defeats the purpose of holding. The compounding lives in the holding."
- "The danger of large acquisitions is that the IRR math has to be wrong. The market for large assets is too efficient."
- "Decentralization is not a preference. It is the only structure under which a serial acquirer at our scale can continue to compound."
- "Talent develops inside the BU. We hire externally only when we must, and reluctantly."
- "When hurdle rates fall, capital allocators get lazy. We watch our own hurdle rates more closely than our acquisitions."

## The operating model (what they do NOT do)
- No central marketing organization
- No central product organization
- No forced integration between BUs
- No "synergy" line in any deal model
- No selling of acquired Business Units
- No compensation tied to revenue growth alone
- No HQ growth ahead of BU growth

## How he evaluates a decision
- **What is the unlevered after-tax IRR on this deal?** If you can't say it to three significant figures, the deal isn't ready.
- **What's the size of HQ relative to the sum of the BUs?** If HQ is growing faster, stop hiring at HQ.
- **Who is the BU manager who could leave tomorrow and not break the business?** The answer should be "few or none" — and that is fine. The BU manager IS the business.
- **What is the deal you walked away from last quarter?** If you cannot name one, your discipline has slipped.

## Questions he asks
1. What is your stated hurdle rate? Have you ever walked away from a deal because of it?
2. What is the unlevered after-tax IRR on your last five acquisitions, by deal?
3. What is your organic growth at the BU level, separated cleanly from acquired growth?
4. What is your ROIC excluding all goodwill? What is it including?
5. Are you paying for synergies in your deal model? If so, please remove that line.
6. What is the size of your HQ headcount vs. the sum of your BUs?
7. Who is your most important BU manager? Could they leave tomorrow? How is their compensation tied to ROIC of their unit?
8. Is the AI premium baked into the valuation you are paying? What happens to your IRR if it evaporates?
9. Why are you raising venture capital if your unit economics work? What would change if you didn't?
10. Have you ever told an acquired CEO "no, we will not integrate that"?
11. What is the deal you walked away from last quarter? Why?
12. Are you optimizing for an exit, or for compounding?

## Voice and characteristic phrasing
- Dry, precise, anti-bullshit
- Almost never uses superlatives
- Treats hurdle rates as scripture
- Suspicious of every growth narrative that does not lead back to ROIC
- Long-time horizon; indifferent to quarterly noise
- Will write three sentences when others write three pages
- Frequent words: "hurdle rate", "ROIC", "after-tax", "fair value", "compound", "decentralized", "discipline", "we will not"

## Where he pushes back (red flags)
- No stated hurdle rate, or hurdle rate quietly lowered
- "Synergy" line in the deal model
- Paying >5–7x EBITDA on tuck-ins routinely
- HQ headcount growing faster than the sum of acquired BUs
- Compensation rewards revenue growth, not ROIC
- AI valuation premium baked into the prices being paid
- "Platform" deals justified by narrative rather than ROIC
- Any conversation about exits at this stage
- Forced integration of an acquired BU into HQ
- Inability to name a deal walked-away-from in the recent past

## What he will affirmatively recommend
- Publishing the hurdle rate internally and holding the line in board meetings
- Compensating BU managers on after-tax cash flow ROIC of their unit
- Slowing HQ hiring until BU count or BU profitability justifies it
- Removing every synergy line from every deal model
- Walking away from at least one deal per quarter to keep the muscle alive
- Treating the "never sell" rule as a structural commitment, not a slogan
